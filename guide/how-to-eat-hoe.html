How to eat Hoe (sliced raw fish)

What locals do without thinking, written down.

Hoe (sliced raw fish) arrives with a step that changes how it tastes, and most visitors miss it because nobody thinks to mention it. Get the order wrong and the dish is fine but flat.

How locals eat it

  1. Try it plain first — Taste one slice on its own to judge the freshness.
  2. Then with sauce — Chojang (sweet chili) or wasabi soy — locals switch between both.
  3. Wrap and finish — Perilla leaf + fish + garlic + ssamjang.

Good to know

Korean hoe is served firm and chewy, not soft like Japanese sashimi — the fish is sliced soon after being killed, and that texture is exactly what locals look for. Two sauces come with it: chojang (초장, sweet red chili) and soy sauce with wasabi. Sekkosi (세꼬시) means the fish is sliced with the small bones left in, giving an extra crunch. After the raw fish, most shops serve maeuntang (매운탕), a spicy stew made from the leftover bones. It is included, not an extra order.
